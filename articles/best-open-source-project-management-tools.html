Best Open Source Project Management Tools in 2024

Every team, from scrappy startups to sprawling enterprises, faces the same fundamental challenge: keeping projects organized, on track, and delivered on time. While commercial tools like Asana, Monday.com, and Jira dominate headlines, a vibrant world of open source alternatives quietly delivers powerful capabilities without the hefty price tags. Open source project management tools have matured dramatically over the past few years, offering feature sets that rival — and sometimes surpass — their proprietary counterparts. Whether you are a freelance developer looking for a simple Kanban board or a project manager overseeing complex workflows across departments, there is an open source solution built for you. The best part? You retain full control over your data, enjoy the freedom to customize every detail, and benefit from passionate communities that continuously push these platforms forward.

Why Choose Open Source Project Management Tools

Cost Savings Without Compromise

The most obvious advantage is financial. Commercial project management platforms often charge per user per month, and those costs scale quickly. A team of fifty people on a premium plan can easily spend thousands of dollars annually. Open source tools eliminate licensing fees entirely. You pay nothing to download, install, and use the software. The money you save can be redirected toward hiring talent, investing in infrastructure, or funding other critical areas of your business.

Full Data Ownership and Privacy

When you use a cloud-based proprietary tool, your project data lives on someone else's servers. For organizations handling sensitive information — healthcare providers, government agencies, financial institutions — this arrangement can be a dealbreaker. Open source tools let you self-host on your own servers, giving you complete ownership and control over every piece of data. You decide who has access, where the data resides, and how it is protected.

Customizability and Flexibility

Proprietary tools lock you into their vision of how project management should work. Open source tools, by contrast, hand you the keys. You can modify the source code, build custom integrations, design unique workflows, and tailor the platform to match the exact needs of your team. This flexibility is invaluable for organizations with non-standard processes that do not fit neatly into off-the-shelf solutions.

Community Driven Innovation

Open source projects thrive on community contributions. Thousands of developers, designers, and project managers worldwide contribute bug fixes, new features, translations, and documentation. This collective effort often results in rapid innovation and software that reflects the real-world needs of actual practitioners rather than the priorities of a corporate product team.

Top Open Source Project Management Tools

OpenProject

OpenProject stands as one of the most comprehensive open source project management platforms available today. Designed for teams that need enterprise-grade functionality, it covers the entire project lifecycle from inception to delivery. The platform supports traditional waterfall methodologies with Gantt charts, agile frameworks with Scrum and Kanban boards, and hybrid approaches that blend both worlds.

Key features include project planning and scheduling, task management, time tracking, budgeting, risk management, agile boards, meeting management, and document storage. OpenProject also offers robust collaboration tools including wikis, forums, and newsfeeds that keep team members aligned and informed.

The community edition is free and feature-rich, while the enterprise edition adds premium features such as custom themes, single sign-on, and professional support. OpenProject integrates well with tools like Git, SVN, and various CI/CD pipelines, making it especially popular among software development teams. Its active community and well-maintained documentation make onboarding straightforward even for teams new to open source software.

Taiga

Taiga has earned a reputation as the go-to open source tool for agile teams. Its clean, intuitive interface makes it a pleasure to use, and its feature set is laser-focused on agile methodologies. Taiga supports both Scrum and Kanban out of the box, with sprint planning, backlog management, user stories, tasks, and issue tracking all beautifully integrated.

One of Taiga's standout qualities is its emphasis on simplicity without sacrificing depth. New users can get up and running within minutes, yet the platform offers enough sophistication to satisfy experienced agile practitioners. Taiga also includes built-in reporting and analytics dashboards that provide real-time visibility into team velocity, sprint progress, and project health.

Taiga can be self-hosted or used through its cloud service. The self-hosted option gives you full control, while the cloud version offers convenience with generous free tiers for small teams. It integrates smoothly with GitHub, GitLab, and Bitbucket, making it a natural fit for development workflows.

Redmine

Redmine is a veteran of the open source project management landscape, having been around since 2006. Its longevity is a testament to its reliability, flexibility, and the strength of its community. Built on Ruby on Rails, Redmine supports multiple projects simultaneously, each with its own set of configurations, members, and trackers.

Redmine's feature list is extensive: issue tracking, Gantt charts, calendars, time tracking, wiki, forums, document management, and role-based access control. It also supports multiple languages and is highly customizable through a rich ecosystem of plugins and themes. Need a specific feature that does not exist? Chances are a community plugin covers it, or you can build one yourself.

While Redmine's interface may feel dated compared to newer tools, recent updates and community themes have modernized its appearance considerably. Its maturity means it is battle-tested in production environments worldwide, from small nonprofits to large corporations. If you value stability, extensibility, and a proven track record, Redmine remains an excellent choice.

Wekan

Wekan is an open source Kanban board that offers a straightforward, Trello-like experience without the vendor lock-in. If your team primarily works with visual task boards, Wekan delivers everything you need in a clean, responsive package. You create boards, add lists, populate them with cards, assign team members, set due dates, and track progress — all through an intuitive drag-and-drop interface.

Despite its simplicity, Wekan packs useful features including swimlanes, checklists, attachments, labels, and card templates. It supports multiple board views, user permissions, and integration with external services. Wekan is lightweight and easy to deploy, running smoothly on modest hardware or in Docker containers.

For teams that want a no-nonsense Kanban tool they can host themselves, Wekan is hard to beat. It is particularly well-suited for small to medium teams that value simplicity and speed over heavyweight project management features.

Focalboard

Focalboard is an open source alternative to tools like Notion, Trello, and Asana, created by the team behind Mattermost. It offers three distinct experiences: a personal desktop productivity app, a server-based team solution, and a Mattermost integration that brings project management directly into your team communication platform.

Focalboard provides multiple view types including Kanban boards, table views, gallery views, and calendar views, allowing teams to visualize their work in whatever format suits them best. Cards are highly customizable with various property types, making it adaptable to different workflows from simple to-do lists to complex project tracking.

The platform is relatively young but growing rapidly, backed by a strong engineering team and an enthusiastic community. Its tight integration with Mattermost makes it an excellent choice for organizations already using that communication platform.

Leantime

Leantime takes a unique approach by combining project management with strategic planning. It is designed specifically for teams that want to connect their high-level goals and objectives with day-to-day task execution. Leantime supports idea management, milestone planning, task boards, timesheets, and retrospectives within a single cohesive platform.

What sets Leantime apart is its focus on the non-project manager. Many team members find traditional project management tools overwhelming with jargon and complexity. Leantime uses plain language and a simplified interface that makes project management accessible to everyone on the team, regardless of their technical background or project management expertise.

How to Choose the Right Tool for Your Team

Assess Your Methodology

The first step is understanding how your team works. If you follow Scrum or Kanban strictly, tools like Taiga or Wekan are natural fits. If you need traditional project planning with Gantt charts and resource management, OpenProject or Redmine will serve you better. Teams that blend methodologies should look for flexible platforms that support multiple approaches simultaneously.

Consider Your Technical Resources

Self-hosting requires technical infrastructure and ongoing maintenance. If your team includes developers comfortable with server administration, self-hosting any of these tools is straightforward. If you lack dedicated IT resources, consider platforms that offer both self-hosted and cloud options, or choose lightweight tools like Wekan that are easy to deploy with Docker.

Evaluate Integration Needs

Your project management tool does not exist in isolation. Consider how it connects with the rest of your technology stack. Development teams should prioritize tools with strong Git integrations. Organizations relying on specific communication platforms should look for tools that connect seamlessly with those systems.

Think About Scale

A tool that works for five people might struggle with five hundred. Consider your growth trajectory and choose a platform that can scale with you. OpenProject and Redmine have proven track records with large organizations, while tools like Wekan and Focalboard excel at smaller scales.

Conclusion

The open source project management ecosystem has never been stronger. From enterprise-ready platforms like OpenProject to elegant agile solutions like Taiga, from battle-tested workhorses like Redmine to focused Kanban tools like Wekan, there is an open source option for virtually every team and workflow. These tools deliver the functionality teams need to plan, execute, and deliver projects successfully — all while offering the freedom, privacy, and cost savings that only open source software can provide.
