Open Source Alternatives to Figma and Photoshop: A Complete Guide for Designers in 2024

The design world has long been dominated by a handful of powerful but expensive proprietary tools. Adobe Photoshop has ruled image editing for over three decades, while Figma has become the undisputed champion of interface design and prototyping. Together, these applications represent a significant monthly investment for freelancers, small agencies, and independent creators who are just starting out. The subscription model, while convenient for some, can quietly drain hundreds of dollars from a designer's budget every single year.

But here is the good news: the open source community has been hard at work building remarkably capable alternatives. These tools are free, transparent, community-driven, and increasingly competitive with their commercial counterparts. Whether you are a student learning design fundamentals, a freelancer watching every dollar, or a professional who simply believes in the philosophy of open source software, there has never been a better time to explore what is available beyond the paywall.

This guide dives deep into the best open source alternatives to both Figma and Photoshop, examining their strengths, limitations, and ideal use cases so you can make an informed decision about which tools deserve a place in your workflow.

Why Consider Open Source Design Tools?

Before exploring specific applications, it is worth understanding why so many designers are making the switch or at least supplementing their toolkits with open source options.

Cost Savings That Add Up

Adobe Creative Cloud subscriptions can cost anywhere from 55 to 90 dollars per month depending on the plan. Figma offers a free tier but charges 12 to 75 dollars per editor per month for professional and organizational plans. Over the course of a year, these costs accumulate into thousands of dollars. Open source tools eliminate this financial burden entirely. The software is free to download, free to use, and free to distribute.

Data Privacy and Ownership

When you use cloud-based proprietary tools, your files and designs often live on someone else's servers. Open source applications typically store files locally by default, giving you complete control over your intellectual property. For designers working on confidential client projects or in regulated industries, this level of data sovereignty is invaluable.

Customization and Community

Open source software can be modified, extended, and improved by anyone. This means that bugs are often fixed quickly, plugins are developed organically, and the community actively shapes the direction of the product. You are not at the mercy of a single corporation's product roadmap.

Best Open Source Alternatives to Figma

Figma revolutionized collaborative interface design with its browser-based, real-time editing experience. Replicating that magic is no small feat, but several open source projects have made impressive strides.

Penpot: The Leading Open Source Design Platform

Penpot has emerged as the most complete open source alternative to Figma. Developed by Kaleidos and backed by a passionate community, Penpot is a web-based design and prototyping platform that emphasizes collaboration, open standards, and interoperability.

What makes Penpot stand out is its use of SVG as its native file format. Unlike Figma, which uses a proprietary format, Penpot stores everything as standard SVG files. This means your designs are never locked into a single application. You can open Penpot files in any SVG-compatible editor, making it truly portable and future-proof.

Penpot supports real-time collaboration, allowing multiple team members to work on the same file simultaneously. It includes features like components, design tokens, flexbox-based layout capabilities, and interactive prototyping. The interface is clean and intuitive, and Figma users will find the transition relatively smooth since many concepts and shortcuts translate directly.

The platform can be used through the hosted version at penpot.app or self-hosted using Docker, giving teams complete control over their infrastructure. Recent updates have brought significant performance improvements, better plugin support, and enhanced prototyping features that make it viable for professional production work.

Akira: A Native Linux Design Tool

Akira is a newer project that aims to be a native Linux alternative for UI and UX design. Built with Vala and GTK, it focuses on providing a fast, lightweight experience tailored for the Linux desktop environment. While it is still in early development and lacks the feature depth of Penpot, Akira represents an important effort to bring native design tooling to the Linux ecosystem.

The project prioritizes speed and simplicity, making it an interesting option for designers who work primarily on Linux and prefer native applications over browser-based tools.

Quant UX: Prototyping Focused

Quant UX is an open source tool that focuses specifically on prototyping and usability testing. While it does not aim to replace the full design capabilities of Figma, it excels at creating interactive prototypes and gathering user feedback. It includes built-in analytics that track where users click and how they navigate through your prototypes, providing valuable data for UX researchers.

Best Open Source Alternatives to Photoshop

Photoshop is an extraordinarily deep application covering photo editing, digital painting, graphic design, and compositing. Replacing it entirely requires a combination of tools, but several open source applications cover significant portions of its functionality.

GIMP: The Veteran Powerhouse

GIMP, which stands for GNU Image Manipulation Program, has been the flagship open source image editor since 1996. It is the tool most commonly mentioned as a Photoshop alternative, and for good reason. GIMP supports layers, masks, advanced selection tools, color correction, filters, and an extensive plugin ecosystem that extends its capabilities even further.

GIMP handles most common photo editing tasks with ease. Color adjustments, cropping, retouching, and compositing are all well-supported. The software supports a wide range of file formats including PSD, which means you can open and work with Photoshop files from clients or collaborators.

The interface has been a point of criticism over the years, but recent versions have made significant strides in usability. GIMP 2.10 and the upcoming GIMP 3.0 bring a modernized interface, improved performance through GEGL processing, better color management, and support for 16-bit and 32-bit color depth.

Where GIMP falls short compared to Photoshop is in areas like non-destructive editing, advanced typography tools, and some of the more specialized features like content-aware fill that Adobe has refined over decades. However, for the vast majority of photo editing workflows, GIMP is more than capable.

Krita: The Digital Artist's Dream

While GIMP focuses on photo manipulation, Krita is purpose-built for digital painting and illustration. Developed by a team of artists and developers, Krita offers an incredibly polished painting experience that rivals commercial applications like Corel Painter and even surpasses Photoshop in many painting-specific workflows.

Krita features over 100 professionally made brushes, advanced brush engines with extensive customization options, layer management, transform masks, HDR painting support, and animation capabilities. The brush stabilizer is excellent for inking, and the wrap-around mode is perfect for creating seamless textures and patterns.

For designers who do significant illustration work alongside their interface or graphic design projects, Krita is an essential addition to the toolkit. It handles the creative, artistic side of image work while GIMP or another tool can handle photo editing and compositing.

Photopea: Browser-Based and Familiar

Photopea deserves special mention even though it is not strictly open source in the traditional sense. Created by Ivan Kutskir, Photopea is a free, browser-based image editor that closely mirrors Photoshop's interface and feature set. It supports PSD, XCF, Sketch, XD, and CDR files, making it one of the most versatile file compatibility stories in the design world.

Photopea runs entirely in your browser with no installation required, making it perfect for quick edits or for use on devices where you cannot install software. It supports layers, masks, smart objects, pen tools, and even some advanced features like content-aware fill. The free version is ad-supported, while a premium subscription removes ads.

Inkscape: Vector Design Powerhouse

While not a direct Photoshop replacement, Inkscape fills a critical gap in the open source design ecosystem as a professional vector graphics editor. It is the open source answer to Adobe Illustrator and handles logo design, icon creation, typography, and vector illustration with impressive depth.

Inkscape supports SVG as its native format, offers powerful path editing tools, layers, clones, gradients, and an extensive extension system. For designers who need to create scalable graphics alongside their raster work, Inkscape is indispensable.

Building an Open Source Design Workflow

The real power of open source design tools emerges when you combine them into a cohesive workflow. A typical professional setup might look like this: Penpot for interface design and prototyping, GIMP for photo editing and compositing, Krita for illustration and digital painting, and Inkscape for vector graphics and logo work.

This combination covers the vast majority of design tasks you will encounter professionally. The learning curve is real, especially if you are transitioning from years of muscle memory in proprietary tools, but the community provides excellent tutorials, documentation, and forums where you can get help.

Recommendations for Different Users

If you are a UI or UX designer primarily looking for a Figma replacement, start with Penpot. It is the most mature and feature-complete option, and the hosted version requires no setup at all. If you need photo editing capabilities, GIMP is the logical starting point, supplemented by Krita if you do any illustration work. For quick edits or situations where you cannot install software, bookmark Photopea.
